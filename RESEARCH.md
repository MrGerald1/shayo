# Part 1: Nigerian alcohol market and business models

## 1. Market size and players

### Revenue and profit, latest full years

**[V] Nigerian Breweries (Heineken-controlled), FY2025:** revenue ₦1.467trn, up 35% from
₦1.084trn. Gross profit ₦565bn, from ₦320bn. Net profit ₦99.1bn, reversing a ₦145.0bn loss in
2024. Operating profit up over 190%. Cost of sales ₦902bn. Finance costs fell 83% after the 2024
rights issue cleared FX debt. Retained earnings still negative after two years of heavy losses.
[Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/02/14/q4-2025-nigerian-breweries-plc-records-n1-467-trillion-revenue/),
[NB Plc](https://www.nbplc.com/nigerian-breweries-plc-records-n1-5-trillion-group-revenue-in-2025/)

**[V] International Breweries (AB InBev), FY2025:** revenue ₦619.04bn, up 26.6% from ₦488.96bn.
PAT ₦50.91bn against a ₦113.61bn loss in 2024. Operating profit ₦82.89bn. No dividend.
[Nairametrics](http://nairametrics.com/2026/02/04/international-breweries-stages-full-year-comeback-posts-n85-1-billion-profit-in-2025/)

**[V] Guinness Nigeria, 18 months to December 2025** (year-end changed after the takeover):
revenue ₦730.8bn, gross profit ₦230.5bn, operating profit ₦89.3bn, profit ₦41.2bn against a
₦54.8bn loss. For the 12 months to June 2025 alone: revenue ₦496.6bn, up 66%, net profit ₦16.2bn.
[Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2026/02/18/guinness-nigeria-delivers-audited-18-month-performance-marked-by-revenue-growth-and-profit-recovery/)

### The Tolaram deal

**[V]** Announced 11 June 2024. Tolaram bought Diageo's 58.02% of Guinness Nigeria at ₦81.60 per
share, a 63% premium, roughly ₦104bn. Diageo kept the Guinness brand and licensed it back
long-term, plus royalties on locally made Diageo spirits and RTDs. Tolaram later went to 70.86%
via the mandatory takeover offer.
[Diageo](https://www.diageo.com/en/news-and-media/press-releases/2024/diageo-transforms-business-model-in-nigeria)

### Market share

**[V] Q1 2024 volume share:** Nigerian Breweries 55.1% (from 52.6%), International Breweries
25.0% (from 23.2%), Guinness 18.8% (down from 23.1%).
[BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/market-intelligence/article/nigerian-brewers-battle-for-market-share-heats-up-as-headwinds-grow/)

**[V] Q1 2025 by revenue:** NB about 61% of the three combined. NB ₦383.6bn, IB ₦173.6bn,
Guinness ₦118.3bn. Market caps on 5 June 2025: IB ₦1.84trn, NB ₦1.76trn, Guinness ₦197bn.
[Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/2025/06/10/who-dominates-nigerias-beer-market-guinness-vs-international-breweries-vs-nigerian-breweries/)

### Growing or shrinking: value up, volume flat to down

**[V]** Brewers repriced aggressively through 2024 and 2025 to recover margin, so the revenue
growth is largely nominal rather than volume. Per-capita beer consumption is declining under
affordability pressure, with consumers trading down to value tiers and switching to substitutes.
Beer fell substantially in volume terms in 2023.
[Vanguard](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/05/breweries-defy-economic-hardship-record-sharp-rise-in-revenue/),
[Euromonitor](https://www.euromonitor.com/alcoholic-drinks-in-nigeria/report)

**[W]** Production volume put at 17.7m hectolitres in 2024, second in Africa behind South
Africa's ~37m hl, with one summary claiming 19.1 to 19.2m hl for 2025. Untraceable to a primary
document and it sits oddly against the per-capita decline. A separate forecast has consumption
falling to 1,450 thousand tonnes by 2028 from 1,730 in 2023, about -2.8% a year. Treat both as
unverified.

**[V]** August 2025: brewers took a delegation to the Trade and Investment Minister seeking
excise reform. The article contains no hard numbers despite its ₦425bn headline, so that figure
should not be cited as sourced. Quoted: Carlos Coutino (IB CEO), Dr Jumoke Oduwole (Minister).
[ThisDay](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2025/08/07/beer-industry-seeks-excise-duty-reform-amid-n425bn-loss-warning/)

### Excise and devaluation timeline

**[V]** Beer and stout excise: ₦40/litre in 2022, raised to ₦75/litre effective 1 June 2023 plus
a new 20% ad valorem component (wines 30%). A ₦100/litre step for 2024 was scheduled.
[TheCable](https://www.thecable.ng/nigerians-to-pay-more-for-beer-as-fg-increases-tax-rate-to-n75-a-litre/)

**[V]** July 2023: Tinubu signed executive orders suspending the excise escalation on locally
manufactured products and deferring the Finance Act commencement to 1 September 2023. The 2023
measures had pointed to a combined ₦300/litre specific rate by 2024, up 114% from ₦140. That
escalation was halted.
[Guardian](https://guardian.ng/news/tinubu-signs-executive-orders-suspends-5-excise-tax-on-telecoms/)

**[V] Current regime, Nigeria Tax Act, from 1 July 2026:** beer and stout ₦72/litre, rising to
₦76 in 2027 and ₦80 in 2028. Wine 25% ad valorem plus ₦70/litre. Spirits 30% plus ₦75/litre
rising to ₦85 by 2028.
[PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/nigeria/corporate/other-taxes)

**[V] FX:** the naira lost more than 40% against the dollar in 2023. Barley, hops, aluminium and
some packaging are imported, so devaluation flowed straight into cost of sales. NB's 2024 rights
issue was specifically about clearing that exposure, which is why finance costs fell 83% in 2025.

**[W]** H1 2026 combined tax expense for the three brewers: ₦112.87bn versus ₦71.39bn in H1
2025, up about 58%.

### Marketing spend

**[V]** NB's disclosure is combined, not separable. Marketing, distribution and administration:
₦127bn (2023) to ₦184bn (9M 2024, +45%), and ₦254bn (FY2024) to ₦364.14bn (FY2025, +43%).
[Marketing Edge](https://marketingedge.com.ng/nigerian-breweries-hits-n1trn-in-2024-revenue-as-marketing-spend-soars/)

**Gap.** No Nigerian brewer discloses a market research line item. Neither does EABL in Kenya.
Advertising, promotion, distribution and admin are one bucket. **[I]** Sizing the research wallet
requires triangulating from the vendor side; any deck claiming "brewers spend ₦X on research" is
invented unless it names a vendor contract.

## 2. Who sells consumption data to Nigerian brewers

**[V] NielsenIQ** has an active Nigeria and West Africa operation with named leadership (Joyce
Nwachukwu, Associate Director West Africa; Faith Wanderi, Market Leader East and West Africa)
and publishes Nigeria retail measurement analysis.
[NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/analysis/2024/nigeria-state-of-the-nation-2023-overview/)

**[V] Frontline Research Group** runs a syndicated monthly retail audit panel in Nigeria, live
since May 2022, covering Abuja, Ibadan, Enugu, Lagos, Owerri and Port Harcourt from a 2021-22
partial census. Measures sales volume and share, value and share, facings share, distribution,
price and merchandising. Sells on cost-sharing across syndicate participants and claims prior
alcoholic beverage audit experience.
[Frontline](https://frontlineresearchgroup.com/exploring-syndicated-retail-census-and-audits-in-nigeria/)

**[V] Kantar and Ipsos** both operate in Nigeria on brand equity, ad effectiveness and
segmentation for multinational FMCG clients. No contract values published.

**[V] GeoPoll** runs SMS and mobile-web panels in Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa with
one-day turnaround. **Pricing starts at $3.00 per completed survey**, varying by question count
and sample size.
[GeoPoll](https://www.geopoll.com/blog/geopoll-on-demand-collect-fast-custom-data-from-africa/)

**[V] Nielsen pricing** is not published. Vendr, which aggregates real negotiated contracts,
reports a **median of $29,051/yr, range $6,870 to $98,935**, with tiers of $50k-$250k (single
market), $250k-$750k (multi-market), $750k+ (enterprise). Multi-year deals 15% to 30% cheaper;
implementation $5k to $50k, often waived.
[Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/buyer-guides/nielsen)

**Gap.** No published evidence of what a Nigerian, Kenyan or South African brewer pays for retail
audit or panel data. Checked from both the client side (annual reports bundle it) and the vendor
side (all custom-quoted). Vendr medians are global and skew to US software buyers.

**[I] Planning inference:** a single-category, single-country syndicated audit subscription in
Nigeria is plausibly a five-figure to low six-figure dollar annual commitment, monthly cadence,
multi-year. **Frontline's syndication model exists precisely because full-cost custom audits
price out most Nigerian buyers.** The panel business is not competing against a fat incumbent
budget; it is competing against a thin one already syndicated to get the price down.

## 3. Untappd for Business and the venue model

**[W] Pricing, per location:** Essentials $899/yr, Premium $1,199/yr. Monthly billing adds about
20%. Essentials caps at 5 users and 20 non-beer menu items; Premium removes both, adds the
Wine-Searcher database and API access. Untappd does not publish these itself, so both sources are
competitors reverse-engineering.
[Brewlytics](https://brewlytics.ai/blog/untappd-for-business-pricing),
[BeerMenus](https://www.beermenus.com/blog/260-untappd-for-business)

**[V] Scale:** nearly 20,000 venues in over 75 countries. Consumer side: over 11 million users
(Next Glass says nearly 12 million registered), more than 1.5 billion consumption moments, 1.5
million unique beverages checked in across 2.6 million locations in 2025.
[Next Glass](https://www.nextglass.co/untappd-for-business/)

**[V] The venue business changed the consumer product.** Venues are marked "Verified" in the
consumer app **only if they pay**, with no free route to verified status. Paying gets menu
publishing, event promotion and venue analytics. The consumer app's venue layer is a paywalled
surface.

**[V] Sequence:** Untappd launched 2010, hit roughly 8 million users and 900 million check-ins,
and launched Untappd for Business only in 2016. Next Glass took investment from Providence
Strategic Growth in April 2020 and acquired Untappd 1 March 2021. Untappd had raised only $6.4m.
[Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/acquisition/next-glass-acquires-untappd--f03c9974)

**[V] BeerBoard** is the closest precedent for "venue tool becomes data business": hardware plus
software metering every ounce through the tap, managing $1bn+ in retail draft sales and 45,000
products, selling pour data back to brewers. BrewDog signed for exactly that. Trend reports drawn
from 400 selected client locations.
[BeerBoard](https://www.beerboard.com/)

**[I]** The uncomfortable lesson: BeerBoard's data is sellable because it is **metered at the
tap, not self-reported by drinkers.** Self-selected check-ins are a different and much weaker
asset.

**[V] Nigerian venue-software competition is already crowded:** Staycore (nightlife-specific,
bottle service, VIP tables), InkeepX, LoungeOS, OyaSync (offline-ready, anti-fraud), Lumi
Business. All pitch on fraud and stock shrinkage, not menu display.
[Staycore](https://staycore.io/insights/nightclub-management-software-in-nigeria/)

## 4. Events and party supply

**[V] For 300 guests:** catering ₦900,000 to ₦2.4m at ₦3,000 to ₦8,000 per head, and **drinks a
separate ₦400,000 to ₦800,000** covering soft drinks, water and limited alcohol. Small chops
₦150k to ₦400k. A whole owambe runs ₦2m to ₦10m+; ₦5m is the common mid-range benchmark for 200
to 250 guests on the Lagos mainland. **Most Nigerian caterers do not include beverages in the
food quote**, which is the structural opening for a drinks-specific supplier.
[WithinNigeria](https://www.withinnigeria.com/2026/06/09/how-much-does-an-owambe-party-cost-in-nigeria-in-2026/)

**Gap.** No credible published rule of thumb for crates per guest at a Nigerian event. Pricing is
thin and **[W]**: a crate of 12 60cl Hero bottles quoted ₦4,000 to ₦4,200; Trophy in a ₦1,500 to
₦2,500 band with the bottle/crate distinction unclear. Wigmore Wholesale and Drink Allotters
publish live wholesale crate pricing and are the right place to get real numbers.

**[I]** Backing out the quantity: an ₦800,000 drinks budget at roughly ₦8,000 to ₦12,000 per
crate retail across beer, malt and minerals is **65 to 100 crates for 300 guests**. Arithmetic on
one verified budget range, not a sourced figure. Validate with five event planners before
building anything on it.

**[W] Market size around it:** the wedding value chain put at ₦1.2trn a year, food and catering
about ₦400bn, aso-ebi about ₦250bn; creative and event services cited at 2.3% of GDP or nearly
₦5trn from a 2023 NBS report that could not be opened. Average couple spends about ₦13m.
Directional only.

**[V] Who supplies events today.** Drinks.ng, founded March 2013 by Lanre Akinlagun with $100,000
of his own money, run initially from his bedroom with deliveries in his own car, explicitly
inspired by the pain of buying bulk liquor at an open-air market on Lagos Island. Claims 40,000+
customers, expanded to Onitsha 2023, apps on iOS and Android, sells from one bottle to 50+ cases,
sub-60-minute delivery in Lagos, Ibadan, Abuja, Port Harcourt. No disclosed institutional rounds.
[How We Made It In Africa](https://www.howwemadeitinafrica.com/e-commerce-in-nigeria-founder-of-drinks-ng-shares-lessons-learnt/146946/)

**[V]** Naija Liquor positions on **party bundles** for events and hospitality. Bottleking.ng and
Barrels.ng sell nationally; Barrels publishes owambe-targeted content. Jumia runs beer, wine and
spirits with nationwide delivery and cash on delivery, but the catalogue is small: 43 spirits and
liquors, 13 products under drinks.

**Gap.** **No Nigerian startup whose product is an event drinks package with a calculator, an
occasion flow, or per-guest sizing.** Naija Liquor's "party bundles" is a distributor's line item,
not a product. Cirkle, Bettaliving and Gigalager returned nothing identifiable, which is itself a
finding.

**[I]** The purchase today is made by phone and WhatsApp to a distributor or through the planner,
with cash or transfer, and the drinks decision is made late and by guesswork. That matches the
₦400k-₦800k band being so wide. Unverified; validate with planners.

[Jumia](https://www.jumia.com.ng/sp-jumia-affiliate/)

## 6. Regulation

### Advertising

**[V] ARCON** (formerly APCON, renamed under the Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria Act
2022) enforces the Nigerian Code of Advertising. Alcohol-specific rules:

- **[V] Article 39.** Alcohol ads shall not be aired **between 6:00am and 8:00pm on radio** and
  **between 6:00am and 10:00pm on television.** Verified against two independent law-firm
  summaries quoting the article directly.
- **[V] Article 41.** Models **under 25** must not be shown drinking alcohol or appearing to.
  (An earlier draft of this file said "under 18." Wrong.)
- **[V] Articles 42 and 44.** No implying alcohol is essential to business or social success.
- **[I]** Every alcohol ad must carry a responsible drinking message, and no placement adjacent
  to youth content. Widely reported, article number not confirmed.
- **[I]** Billboards need the disclaimer and cannot sit in restricted zones near schools.

**This is a watershed, not a prohibition.** A brewer may advertise on Nigerian television after
10pm. Which is why Trophy and Guinness are visible around late football and BBNaija.

**[?] Which edition is in force.** The Article 39 hours are quoted from the **5th edition,
S.I. No. 64**, which the **6th edition repealed**. Whether the 6th edition carried the hours
over unchanged is unconfirmed. ARCON does not publish the Code text on its own site: the code
page carries no document link, and `advertcouncil.gov.ng/documents/` serves only `ARCON LAW.pdf`
(the Act, not the Code). Getting the current text means a request to ARCON or a paid legal
database. **Do not quote these hours to a brewer without that.**

Described as among the more strictly enforced provisions, on a secondary source rather than
the code text.

**[?] Open.** Brewer advertising is visible around Supersport football and BBNaija, which is
consistent with post-watershed placement, but two things are unchecked: whether sponsorship and
in-show brand integration are treated as a separate category from spot advertising, and how
tightly the 6am to 10pm window is actually enforced. Read the code text before quoting these
hours at a brewer.

**[!] There is no outright ban on television alcohol advertising in Nigeria.** An earlier draft
of SCOPE.md and the CEO plan claimed one. Corrected 13 September 2026.
[Nigerian Code of Advertising](https://advertcouncil.gov.ng/_nig_code_of_advertising.php)

**[V]** ARCON's 2023 vetting guidelines require **pre-vetting of advertising material**, and
ARCON asserts jurisdiction over foreign companies advertising into Nigeria.

**[I]** The time-of-day rules are written for broadcast and outdoor and do not map cleanly onto
an app. But comedic roasts about drinks distributed as social content is advertising-adjacent
material ARCON would plausibly claim jurisdiction over if a brewer ever paid for placement in it.
**The moment money flows from a brewer, the pre-vetting question becomes live.**

### Sachet ban

**[V]** Senate ordered NAFDAC to ban sachet alcohol production by December 2025. NAFDAC
reaffirmed. **The ban took effect 1 January 2026**, covering sachets, PET below 200ml and glass
below 200ml, enforced first at manufacturing level. NAFDAC has since ordered nationwide recall
and physical destruction, with threats of permanent facility closure and prosecution.
[NAFDAC](https://nafdac.gov.ng/press-release-by-director-general-national-agency-for-food-and-drug-administration-and-control-prof-mojisola-christianah-adeyeyenafdac-reaffirms-commitment-to-enforce-the-ban-on-alcohol-in-sachets-a/)

**[V]** Still contested. SERAP filed an originating summons dated 15 December 2025 over
enforcement delay. On 23 February 2026 the Federal Ministry of Health filed a counter-affidavit
saying only NAFDAC has enforcement power. **[V]** Compliance is poor; sachet alcohol was still on
sale five months after enforcement began.

### The new national policy, the one to watch

**[V] The Nigeria Alcohol Policy and Multisectoral Implementation Plan (2026-2030)**, unveiled
**27 August 2026** by the Federal Ministry of Health and Social Welfare:

- Every producer, importer, distributor and **retail outlet must be licensed in a national
  digital registry, with age verification as a condition of the licence.**
- Alcohol-free zones of 200 metres around schools, health facilities and motor parks.
- The sachet and sub-200ml prohibition written into national policy.
- **Excise duties maintained, not raised.** With inflation above 20%, alcohol gets cheaper in
  real terms each year.
- Marketing rules address only targeting of minors and proximity to schools. **Digital marketing
  is largely untouched**, even though the government acknowledges digital alcohol promotion to
  young people is growing. No limits on broadcast, outdoor, sport or music sponsorship, and no
  price promotion limits.
[Movendi](https://movendi.ngo/policy-updates/2026/08/30/nigeria-launches-new-national-alcohol-policy/)

**[V]** Legal drinking age is 18.

**[I]** The registry-plus-age-verification requirement attaches to **retail outlets**, meaning
anyone selling. **If Shayo ever takes an order for drinks, it is arguably in scope. A media
property or a habit tracker that never touches a transaction is not.** This is the sharpest
regulatory difference between the strategic options, and it is brand new, so untested.

### Meta and WhatsApp

**[V] Meta advertising:** alcohol ads are prohibited in Afghanistan, Brunei, Bangladesh, Egypt,
Gambia, certain Indian states, Kuwait, Libya, Lithuania, Nepal, Norway, Pakistan, Russia, Saudi
Arabia, Thailand, Turkey, UAE and Yemen. **Nigeria is not on the prohibited list.** Default
minimum targeting age 18.
[Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/alcohol/)

**[V] WhatsApp, the important one.** From **27 August 2024**, alcohol businesses were permitted to
use the WhatsApp Business Platform for customer engagement in **India, APAC and LATAM** (one
summary names Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Mexico, South Korea, Vietnam; promotion remains
prohibited in India). **Commerce stays banned everywhere:** any messaging related to buying,
selling and paying for alcohol is governed by the Meta Commerce Policy and remains disallowed
**across all WhatsApp surfaces**. Alcohol companies may not use Commerce Catalogs, Payments, or
any other commerce experience. Messaging in regulated verticals may not be sent to under-18s.
Repeat violators get escalating send restrictions.
[Hubtype](https://www.hubtype.com/blog/whatsapp-policy-update-gambling-otc-drugs-alcohol)

**Gap and warning.** whatsapp.com/legal/commerce-policy and business.whatsapp.com/policy could
not be fetched; both 301 to whatsappbusiness.com which refused the connection. The regional list
comes from vendor blogs, not Meta. **No source confirms the alcohol messaging allowance extends
to Africa or EMEA at all.** **[I]** Until confirmed in writing from Meta or a BSP, assume alcohol
brand messaging on WhatsApp in Nigeria is unapproved and alcohol commerce on WhatsApp is flatly
prohibited. **This is a hard constraint on any WhatsApp-channel plan.**

## 7. Companies that sold alcohol consumption data

**[V] Untappd Insights** sells to breweries, distributors, retailers and beverage marketers on
nearly 12 million registered users and over 1.5 billion consumption moments: trending styles by
market, competitive performance, product-market fit validation, engagement measurement,
go-to-market guidance, as reports and dashboards. **No pricing, no sample sizes, no named
customers disclosed.** 8 million users and 900 million check-ins accumulated before the data
product existed; the B2B venue product came six years after the consumer app.
[Next Glass](https://www.nextglass.co/untappd-insights/)

**[V] Vivino:** $491m in net sales on vivino.com in 2025, projected up 5% to 10% in 2026. Over 50
million users, nearly 79 million reviews on more than 14 million wines, 231,000+ wineries.
Revenue is mostly marketplace commission and direct sales, plus subscriptions and advertising,
**with aggregated rating data sold to other businesses as a secondary line.**
[Statista](https://www.statista.com/forecasts/1546802/vivino-e-commerce-revenue-global)

**[V] Drizly** launched a data and insights platform for bevalc retailers in 2020. BevAlc Insights
published free weekly trend reporting plus paid data add-ons and custom packages; the annual
Retail Report surveyed 500+ independent liquor store owners including non-Drizly retailers. Later
added Drizly Ads, full-funnel advertising connecting brands to buyers, on-site and offsite. Uber
bought Drizly for $1.1bn in 2021 and shut it down: announced January 2024, dead end of March
2024, consolidated into Uber Eats. Context: pandemic sales ran up to 400% above historical, and a
2020 breach exposed about 2.5 million customers from a flaw known for over two years.
[CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/01/15/business/uber-is-shutting-down-drizly/index.html)

**[I] What it means.** Drizly was absorbed, not killed by a failed data product. The harsher
reading: **in every case the data was a by-product of a transaction or a metered pour, never the
reason users showed up.** Vivino sells wine. Drizly sold liquor. BeerBoard meters kegs. Untappd
had 8 million people logging beers socially for six years before anyone monetised the exhaust,
and Insights still has no published price, which usually means small and bespoke.

**[I] On "how many users before the data is sellable":** no published threshold exists. Untappd's
history is the only real data point and it says **eight figures of users and nine figures of
check-ins over six years.** A Nigerian panel would need far fewer to be statistically useful, but
it would need to be **representative**, and self-selected users of a comedy app are not. No amount
of user growth fixes that.

## 8. Price indices as brands

**[V] Big Mac Index:** created by The Economist in 1986, developed by editor Pam Woodall,
originally a joke to explain purchasing power parity, now treated as a real exchange-rate
evaluation technique. Works because McDonald's is everywhere and the product is near-identical,
so its price absorbs local ingredient costs, wages and advertising. **The Economist gives it
away:** methodology and code on GitHub under MIT, data under CC-BY 4.0. No licensing revenue. It
is a marketing asset for the masthead.
[TheEconomist/big-mac-data](https://github.com/theeconomist/big-mac-data)

**[V] Jollof Index:** built by SBM Intelligence, launched 2015, price collection started July
2016. Tracks the cost of ingredients for a standardised pot of jollof rice for a family of five:
rice, tomatoes, onions, pepper, seasoning, protein, vegetable oil. Collected monthly from **13
markets in Nigeria and two in Ghana**, excluding December for seasonality, **published quarterly
without a gap since.** Rebased and redefined in Q2 2026.
[SBM Q2 2026](https://www.sbmintel.com/2026/07/the-sbm-jollof-index-q2-2026-rebasing-redefining-and-the-weathers-toll-on-the-pot/)

**[V]** SBM Intelligence, founded 2012, sells market intelligence, security analysis and
strategic consulting, plus advice on data collection and survey quality, plus strategic
communication and government relations. Clients span governments, multilaterals, FMCG companies,
tech startups, investors, insurers and media. The index gets picked up internationally, including
by Quartz Africa on food inflation and the naira.

**Gap.** SBM publishes no revenue figures and there is no public account of what the Jollof Index
did for its P&L.

**[I]** The observable pattern: the index is free, quarterly, ten years old, and functions as the
lead generator for paid consulting, exactly as the Big Mac Index does for The Economist. Both say
the same thing. **A recurring index is not a product you sell, it is a reputation you spend.**
Monetisation is downstream, in advisory and access, and it takes years of unbroken cadence before
anyone cites you without prompting.

# Part 2: Product comparables and jobs to be done

Research constraints: Untappd's help center returns 403 to automated fetches, so its check-in
steps come from its product blog, Wikipedia, Google Play and press coverage. **No vendor in this
set publishes tap counts or time-to-log numbers.** No documented Nigerian equivalent of Wrapped
from a local app exists publicly; Boomplay and Audiomack run year-end recaps as marketing charts,
not per-user shareable cards.

## Untappd

**The check-in flow.** Documented paths: open a beer page and tap Check-In; at a Verified Venue
tap "Check-In Here" and pick from the live menu, which stamps location automatically; tap a Quick
Check-In icon on a beer card in search results; or scan a UPC or QR code. **On the check-in sheet
everything is optional:** rating, photo, tasting notes, serving style (draft, can, bottle, and
since July 2024 side pull, tank, slushie), friends tagged, venue, purchase venue, flavour tags.
Untappd states explicitly you do not have to leave a rating.

**[I]** The floor is roughly two deliberate taps from a venue menu; the ceiling is a long-form
review. **That optionality is the design: the fast path and the rich path are the same screen.**

**Rating:** a slider 0 to 5 in 0.25 increments; paid Insiders get 0.1. A drag, not a keyboard
entry, which is why it survives one-handed use.

**The reward: badges.** Over 15,000 badges have existed or are in play; the community passed its
billionth badge in 2022. Three types: core (always available), sponsored (brewery-funded,
time-boxed), local (venue loyalty). Levelled badges advance every 5 qualifying check-ins up to
level 100, which is 500 beers. April 2025 extended core milestones to 40,000 unique beverages.
**Users already past a threshold get the badge automatically on their next check-in** — the
reward is retroactive and arrives attached to the next log, never as a separate errand. Untappd's
own data: **sponsored badges lift check-ins 50 to 100% for the sponsoring brand** during the
promotion.
[Untappd Lounge](https://lounge.untappd.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-untappd-badges/)

**Shareable artefacts.** Per check-in: a card with beer name, brewery, star rating, photo, notes,
plus toasts and comments. Help docs say social sharing needs no account linking; **it copies your
check-in details to the clipboard for pasting.** Annually: Year in Beer, since 2024 an animated
story view, vertical stat cards, one-button share, released alongside four weekly batches of
Style Hunt badges from 2 to 29 December, each unlock giving a prize-draw entry.

**Why it won.** Greg Avola built the prototype in under 24 hours and launched two weeks later on
about $1,000. Neither founder quit their job for five years. Growth was unpaid; a Mashable
write-up was the tipping point. 1M users January 2014, 3.2M April 2016, close to 9M users and
925M+ check-ins at ten years, 820,000 monthly actives across 180 countries reported September
2020. **The differentiator against rival beer apps was that the check-in was social and gamified
rather than a private cellar log, and monetisation moved off the drinker entirely onto venues,
which made venue menus the fastest logging surface.** The data and the convenience reinforce each
other.
[Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/201909/cameron-albert-deitch/untappd-greg-avola-tim-mather-beer-social-foursquare-2019-inc5000.html)

## Vivino

**Scan to verdict.** Point at a label, get one page with the community rating (1 to 5 with review
count), price range, tasting-note breakdown, food pairings, and a **Match for You** percentage.
On iPhone this also fires through Apple Visual Intelligence without opening the app.

**Personal versus crowd, kept visually distinct.** The crowd number is 1 to 5 stars with the
review count as its warrant. Match for You is 0 to 100%, computed only from your own rating
history across grapes, styles, regions and flavour profiles. Stated bands: 70-100% likely to
please, 40-70% average and worth a try, under 40% probably not for you. Two people see different
Match scores on the same bottle. **Community data is a quality filter in the recommender, not an
input to your Match.**
[Vivino](https://www.vivino.com/en/wine-news/vivinos-match-for-you-score-explained-how-we-learn-your-taste)

**Cold start.** Match works after the first rating, but Vivino says **five ratings gives a
starting point and 50 to 100 gives precision.** Before that you can seed it manually by marking
grapes and regions you love or dislike. A declared preference substituting for behavioural data.

**Its empty state.** When the scanner fails (small producers, new releases, worn labels), it
routes to typed search first. If genuinely absent you submit the label and **Vivino's team
reviews it, typically adding a match within a few hours**, after which every future scan resolves
instantly. **The empty state is framed as a contribution with a stated turnaround, not an error.**

## Cal AI

**Speed comes from removing search.** Four entry modes: photo, barcode, nutrition-label capture,
voice ("two scrambled eggs on sourdough, half an avocado"). Snap, review, save. Reviewers describe
it as faster than searching a database and adjusting serving sizes. **The documented weak spot is
correction: fixing a misidentified item takes several steps**, and the database is smaller than
MyFitnessPal's, so branded and regional foods land badly. Roughly 500,000 monthly installs and
about $2M monthly revenue, behind a 28-step quiz onboarding and a 3-day trial soft paywall.
[Screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/cal-ai-calorie-tracker)

**Running total.** One ring showing **calories left rather than consumed** (example: 860 of 2,200
kcal), macro bars underneath, then water, steps, streak, then today's meals. Swipe reveals fibre
and sodium. **The headline number is a remainder, which is a subtraction the user never performs.**

**Monthly view.** Trends with 7D/30D/90D/1Y weight charts, a written weekly summary, a
days-on-target count, and a projection of when you reach the goal at the current pace. A separate
Milestones tab of animated badges. **The framing device is days-on-target plus trajectory rather
than a per-day pass or fail.**

**The contrast case: MyFitnessPal.** A BJPsych Open qualitative study found users felt guilt,
embarrassment and shame at the red visualisation for exceeding the budget, and critics note the
number goes red identically whether you are over by 1 calorie or 1,000. **Roughly 30% of eating
disorder patients in one survey said they used the app and believed it contributed.** Cal AI has
no documented share card for a day's log; its social artefact is milestone badges.
[BJPsych Open](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bjpsych-open/article/effects-of-diet-and-fitness-apps-on-eating-disorder-behaviours-qualitative-study/2D1EE739D97AB3EFC6573835E4C527BD)

## Spotify Wrapped and the shareable card

Analyses converge: **the card gets posted because it is about the poster, not the brand.** Three
mechanics do the work: rank statements that confer status ("top 0.5% of listeners"), identity
labels announceable as a category you belong to (Listening Personalities), and group membership
(Sound Towns). Each slide is pre-sized for vertical feeds so no cropping stands between seeing
and posting. Academic framing from Manchester and LSE work: Wrapped is an "algorithmic event", a
synchronised moment when everyone orients to the same system at once, converting individual cards
into a feed-wide ritual. Spotify said 2024 triggered a record number of shares.

**The uncomfortable half, from the same research:** people post it to see an aspirational version
of themselves reflected back, so **the card that flatters gets posted and the card that merely
reports does not.**
[LSE Impact](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/impactofsocialsciences/2025/01/30/how-algorithmic-events-like-spotify-wrapped-can-reveal-what-tech-companies-really-know-about-us/)

## Splitwise

**Itemised layout.** Each receipt line listed with its price and the person assigned; the
per-person breakdown shows food subtotal, proportional tax, proportional tip, final total.
**Proportionality is the fairness rule that makes it arguable: 30% of the subtotal means 30% of
tax and tip.** Expense rows carry date, amount and payer. Colour does the owed/owes work: green
owed to you, red owed by you.

**What it gets right at settle-up.** Two profile pictures on opposite sides of a central arrow.
**The direction of money is spatial, not textual, so it reads without parsing.**

**What it gets wrong.** Debt simplification aggregates obligations to minimise payment count, and
users report being startled to owe one person when they thought they owed three, **with no
explanation on screen.** Tapping "More options" abandons the arrow diagram for plain name lists,
so choices must be held in memory across screens. "Plus 2 other balances" looks expandable and is
not. Small input fields. **Strong at two-party direction, weak the moment the maths becomes
non-obvious.**
[Pratt critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-splitwise-mobile-app/)

## Non-punitive streak apps

**Finch.** The bird never dies, never leaves, and nothing is lost by missing days. An explicit
guilt-free streak pause. Currency (rainbow stones) is earned, never deducted. The comparison
writers draw is to Habitica, which gamifies achievement and punishes; **Finch gamifies
self-compassion.**

**Gentler Streak.** 2024 Apple Design Award winner. Slogan: move consistently, not constantly. A
logged rest day, sick day or holiday **counts toward the streak instead of breaking it**, via a
"Go Gentler" button letting you declare rest, active recovery, strength or cooldown. Reviewers
note the copy avoids "you failed your goal" language. **The mechanic: the bad week is
representable inside the system rather than showing up as an absence. A gap in the chart reads as
failure; a labelled rest day does not.**
[gentler.app](https://gentler.app/)

## The six surfaces, distilled

| Surface | Solved well by | The mechanic | Solved badly by | The convention already in the head |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Round logger** | Untappd venue menu | The bar's tap list *is* the grid; everything but identity optional | Search-first loggers; Cal AI's correction path | A grid of large square tiles, count badge incrementing in place |
| **Running total** | Cal AI | The number is what is *left*, pre-doing the arithmetic | MyFitnessPal: red at 1 over and at 1,000 over | Big number on top, smaller list beneath that sums to it, tap to expand |
| **Settle receipt** | Splitwise itemised mode | Proportional allocation, stateable in one sentence. Two avatars and an arrow | Splitwise debt simplification: a correct total whose derivation is invisible | The paper till receipt. Left item, right price, rule, bold total, monospaced digits |
| **Ledger** | Gentler Streak, Finch | A rest day is a logged labelled state that counts | MyFitnessPal: binary colour on a continuous quantity | A month grid with intensity encoding volume. Read for pattern, not score |
| **Radar empty** | Vivino, GasBuddy | Submission with a named beneficiary and a stated turnaround; points and a daily draw | Numbeo: withholds below a threshold it never publishes, so nobody contributes | Progress toward a threshold with a count: "3 of 5 reports" |
| **Share card** | Wrapped, Untappd Year in Beer | Pre-sized vertical, rank plus identity plus group, one-button share | Untappd per-check-in: copies to clipboard to paste yourself | 9:16, 1080x1920, one dominant statement, readable at arm's length, no crop |

## WhatsApp status in Nigeria

**[V] Reach.** WhatsApp is used by **96.5% of Nigerian internet users aged 16+** in a given month,
leads on daily use, and is the self-declared favourite platform of 33.9%. It is the default
distribution layer, not one channel among several.
[Krestel Digital](https://kresteldigital.com/resources/social-media-statistics-in-nigeria-2025-data-report/)

**[V] What circulates.** Small-business flyers (cakes, thrift wear, hair), memes and skit clips,
football and betting codes, BBNaija and celebrity gist, church and event flyers, screenshots of
tweets. WhatsApp TV channels exist purely as status-first publishers and run airtime giveaways to
hold viewership. **Status Reshare**, rolled out 2025, lets viewers repost someone else's status to
their own, which Fact Check Africa credits with accelerating spread among Nigerians.

**[V] Visual properties those images share:**

- **9:16 vertical, authored at 1080x1920.** Anything else letterboxes.
- **Text large enough to read without tapping in.** Designed to be understood from the thumbnail
  and the first half-second, because viewers tap through fast.
- **High contrast, usually saturated flat backgrounds with heavy display type.** This survives
  WhatsApp's recompression, which **downscales to roughly 1600px on the long edge at about 60 to
  70% JPEG quality with 4:2:0 chroma subsampling and strips metadata.**
- **Safe margins.** The top and bottom ~250px are occupied by the profile chip and caption.
- **Self-contained.** Links are uncommon. Contact details are **burned into the image** as a phone
  number or handle rather than a tappable link, because the image is expected to be re-forwarded
  away from its origin.
- **Under about 1MB** is the practical target for quality retention; WhatsApp accepts up to 16MB
  but recompresses regardless.
- **Forwarding friction:** capped at five chats at a time, once-forwarded messages go to one
  group only, and anything forwarded five or more times is labelled "Forwarded many times".

[moda.app status sizes](https://moda.app/resources/sizes/whatsapp-status),
[WhatsApp on forwarding](https://blog.whatsapp.com/labeling-forwarded-messages)

**[V] Data prices are a live constraint.** MTN's cheapest monthly plan went from ₦1,000 for 1.2GB
to ₦1,500 for 2GB in February 2025, and its 15GB plan tripled from ₦2,000 to ₦6,000.
[NaijaTechGuide](https://www.naijatechguide.com/mtn-data-plans-in-nigeria-with-prices.html)
