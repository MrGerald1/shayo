# The boundary

What a brand can buy in Shayo, and what no amount of money buys. Written before there is a
cheque on the table, which is the only time it can be written honestly.

`SCOPE.md` states the rule. This file makes it something you can put in front of an agency.

---

## The rule

**Drawn by surface, not by intent.** An earlier draft forbade selling the ordering of search
results and then permitted category takeovers in the picker, which are the same thing wearing a
different name. Intent is unfalsifiable. A surface is either for sale or it is not, and anyone
can check.

### Never sellable. No exceptions, no price.

- **Every roast.** All 73 lines, and every line added after them.
- **Every verdict.** Oya go, Small small, You never reach, You don pass am.
- **The tolerance answer.** What shelf you are on and whether you can handle a drink.
- **Search order, feed order, and the contents of the feed.**
- **Ranking or ordering anywhere in the product.**
- **The Shayo Index**, every issue, including which SKUs it covers.
- **Any text a user could read as a joke.**

A sponsored challenge carries roast-like content, so it sits on this side of the line unless the
copy is written by Shayo and marked as such.

### Sellable. Always visually distinct, always marked.

- **Dedicated slots that reorder nothing.** A slot is a slot. It does not move a drink up the
  feed, into the openers, or to the top of a search result.
- **Seasonal editions.** A named, dated, marked edition that is obviously a collaboration.
- **Activations at events.** Physical, off-product, where nobody mistakes it for editorial.

---

## Why this is structural rather than a preference

The audience exists because the jokes are believed. A roast about Alomo is funny because nobody
paid for it. The moment a reader suspects a line was bought, every other line becomes suspect,
including the 72 that were not. You cannot un-ring that.

The same logic kills the panel: research nobody believes is worth nothing to the brewer who
bought it, so selling the jokes destroys the asset you were selling access to.

**This is not a promise to be nice. It is the reason the thing has value.**

---

## How a marked slot must look

Marking is not a disclosure line in 11px grey. A slot is marked when somebody scrolling at speed
can tell without reading:

- Different ground. Never the drink's own colour, never paper, never ink.
- The word **Advert** at label size, not "partner", "presented by", "in collaboration with", or
  anything else that is trying not to be read as an advert.
- No roast voice. Brand copy in brand voice, or Shayo copy that is clearly labelled Shayo copy.
- Never inside a status card, and never on a card that can be shared, because a card that
  travels loses its context in one forward.

If a buyer asks for it to look more native, the answer is no, and that is what the boundary is.

## ARCON

**This is a blocker on the first conversation, not an open question.**

ARCON's 2023 vetting guidelines require advertising material to be pre-vetted, and ARCON asserts
jurisdiction over foreign companies advertising into Nigeria. The moment money flows from a
brewer, anything Shayo publishes on their behalf is advertising material and the pre-vetting
question is live.

The time restrictions in `RESEARCH.md` (Article 39: radio 6am to 8pm, television 6am to 10pm)
are written for broadcast and outdoor and do not map onto an app. Digital being unaddressed is
the opening, not a licence. Do not quote those hours to a brewer without the current Code text,
which ARCON does not publish and which the 6th edition may have changed.

**Before the first brand conversation:** confirm what ARCON considers vettable in an app, and
what the turnaround is. Not after.

## The refusal

Have this ready, because the request will be reasonable and the person asking will be pleasant.

> We sell slots, not lines. Nothing written in the app's voice is for sale, including anything
> that reads like a joke, and nothing reorders. That rule is why the audience believes the app,
> which is what you are actually buying. Here is what the slot looks like.

If that loses the deal, it loses the deal. A brand that needs the jokes is buying the one thing
that stops working the moment it is sold.

## When any of this applies

Not yet. `SCOPE.md` defers selling anything until an audience floor set at launch, or a named
inbound. At zero users a boundary document is cheap insurance; a rate card is a fantasy.
